THE NASA FOOTAGE OF THE SUN — SDO / AIA 304 Å

This note describes real footage of the Sun from NASA's Solar Dynamics
Observatory. The site showed it until October 2026 and can be switched back
to it. The label beside the Sun says which one the page is showing: if it
reads "Rendered by me in Python · based on NASA SDO", the picture is a computer model
that was calibrated against this footage, and a note of its own describes
it.

Credit: NASA's Goddard Space Flight Center/SDO.
Source: Filament Eruption Creates 'Canyon of Fire' on the Sun, SVS 11379.
https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/11379/

Standalone 304 channel, not the narrated film or composite:
https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a010000/a011300/a011379/SDO_2013-09-29_304_4k.mov

Genuine false-color extreme-ultraviolet observation time-lapse from
September 29–30, 2013. Not live, real-time, or visible-light footage.
No simulated surface motion or generated plasma. The footage does not turn:
neither the files nor the page turn the picture. What moves in it is the
real Sun's own motion, sped up by the time-lapse.

Source: 4096 × 4096 ProRes, 30 fps.
Selected interval: source playback 10–18 seconds; frames 300–539 inclusive.
Local delivery: 1280 × 1280 (a 2048 × 2048 encode is kept in the source code
but not published, since the Sun is shown at most 200 pixels across), H.264,
30 fps, 240 frames, eight seconds, muted and inline. The still shown while
the footage loads, for reduced motion, and after a media failure is the first
frame of the delivered video (WebP). Footage paused by the visitor holds the
frame it had reached.

The native video plays at the published source pace, so one loop takes
eight seconds. It continues independently of scrolling or pointer input.
The return to the first frame is an edited cut, not a seamless physical event.

Treatment: bounded extraction from the 4K master, Lanczos downsampling,
high-quality intermediate encodes, then web compression and fast-start
metadata. Original false-color palette retained. No crop, rotation,
recoloring, masking, synthetic motion, frame interpolation, or added glow.
The low-resolution preview is not shipped. All 240 delivered frames were
checked for gaps and edge clipping; an earlier candidate containing a
source-damaged frame at 18.2667 seconds was rejected.

The entire media square is shown, through a soft round mask that lets the
black sky go, fixed in the window's upper-right corner. Display size is capped
by source resolution and device pixel ratio.

When the footage is the Sun on show, these come from its first delivered
frame: the link-preview image (the unaltered frame beside the site's own
text), and the browser icons (square crop around the disk, Lanczos downsampling, black sky
converted to transparency from pixel brightness at the small sizes). Nothing in them is recolored, sharpened, or generated.
